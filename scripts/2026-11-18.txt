This is India News Today with Nisha. It's Wednesday, November eighteenth.

Another cyclone is headed for India's east coast. The India Meteorological Department says Cyclone Akvan, now over the Bay of Bengal, is likely to become a very severe cyclonic storm by Thursday. It is expected to cross the Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Visakhapatnam around noon on Friday, November twentieth, with winds of one hundred and twenty to one hundred and thirty kilometres an hour, gusting to one hundred and forty-five. An orange alert is in place for Andhra Pradesh and the adjoining south Odisha coast.

India has condemned a missile attack on an LPG tanker in the Strait of Hormuz. The Ministry of External Affairs says the Panama-flagged MT Tara Gas, with twenty-four Indians on board, was hit on November sixteenth. Twenty-one were evacuated ashore in Oman, two injured crew members are in hospital, and one Indian national is still missing. The ministry said the targeting of commercial shipping must end.

In New York, Congress leader Rahul Gandhi told an audience at Columbia University that India is sitting on an economic disaster, pointing to unemployment and the education system, and said his party has evidence of electoral malpractice. The BJP called his remarks anti-India and accused him of defaming the country abroad.

Troubled airline SpiceJet left hundreds of passengers stranded overnight at Mumbai airport after cancelling a dozen flights. On Wednesday, its board approved a plan to raise up to fifty-five hundred crore rupees, and said it is talking to potential new investors. Government data show SpiceJet's domestic market share fell to just zero point six percent in September.

On Dalal Street, stocks pulled back from nine-month highs on profit-taking, with metal, auto and IT shares leading the decline. The Sensex fell five hundred points to eighty-two thousand and thirty-two. The Nifty lost one hundred and fifty-nine points to twenty-five thousand, two hundred and twenty-eight. The rupee closed little changed at ninety-three point four four to the dollar.

In entertainment, Ramayana Part One keeps breaking records. Trade tracker Sacnilk puts its worldwide gross at about fourteen hundred and eighty-three crore rupees after thirteen days, and it is the first Hindi film to gross more than fifty crore rupees in each of the four big southern markets. Also, Nawazuddin Siddiqui and Dia Mirza will star in Doctor Hassan, an Indian adaptation of the American medical drama House, coming to JioHotstar on December eleventh.

And in cricket, India begin a two-Test series against New Zealand on Thursday at the Basin Reserve in Wellington. Captain Shubman Gill says India will play four fast bowlers on a grassy pitch. New Zealand, who top the World Test Championship table, are without Mitchell Santner, who fractured his right hand, while Rachin Ravindra returns from a shoulder injury. Bowling coach Morne Morkel said India know they are better than some of their recent results.
