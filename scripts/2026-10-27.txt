Cyclone Arnab is closing in on India's east coast. The India Meteorological Department says the very severe cyclonic storm was about one hundred and forty kilometers from Kakinada on Tuesday afternoon, and is expected to cross the Andhra Pradesh coast just south of Yanam by Wednesday forenoon, with winds gusting to one hundred and fifty kilometers an hour. A storm surge of one to two meters could flood low-lying areas. Nearly eighty-nine thousand people on the Kakinada coast are being moved to relief camps, Odisha plans to move forty-eight thousand, at least seventy-two trains have been cancelled and Visakhapatnam's beaches are closed.

The Election Commission has told the Supreme Court that all its decisions on the special intensive revision of voter rolls were taken unanimously by its three members, rejecting claims that Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar acted alone. Congress says unanimity does not prove the rules were followed, and Rahul Gandhi is urging supporters to join a Save the Vote rally at Delhi's Ramlila Maidan on November first. Separately, Sonia Gandhi says there is bound to be some tamasha when her memoir, Belonging, comes out on November tenth.

The Supreme Court has agreed to hear fresh challenges to the law requiring all six stanzas of Vande Mataram to be sung, and has set a hearing for November thirtieth.

Indian stocks ended almost flat in a volatile session, weighed by crude oil near one hundred and sixteen dollars a barrel and caution ahead of the U.S. Federal Reserve's decision. The Sensex slipped eight points to seventy-three thousand, five hundred and ninety-nine, and the Nifty dipped about three points to twenty-two thousand, seven hundred and fifty. Smaller companies did much better, with the Nifty Smallcap one hundred up two point seven percent. The rupee edged up three paise to ninety-five point one two to the dollar.

In entertainment, actor Imran Khan, a U.S. citizen who holds an Overseas Citizen of India card, has won temporary relief. He had been told to leave India by October twenty-eighth, but the Bombay High Court has put off hearing his challenge until November second after the government sought more time.

In cricket, Abhishek Sharma smashed a thirty-five-ball century in Wellington as India beat New Zealand by six wickets in the third T twenty, taking an unbeatable three-nil lead in the five-match series. New Zealand made one hundred and ninety-eight for three. Abhishek made one hundred and thirteen off thirty-eight balls with eleven sixes, and he and Vaibhav Sooryavanshi put on one hundred and thirty-three for the first wicket as India got home in just thirteen point two overs. It was Abhishek's eleventh T twenty hundred, the most by any Indian, one more than Virat Kohli.
