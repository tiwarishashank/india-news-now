This is India News Today with Nisha. It's Monday, November twenty-third.

India has announced a development package of about fifteen thousand crore rupees for Nepal, as Prime Minister Narendra Modi hosted Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah in New Delhi. The package includes three thousand five hundred crore rupees in grants for post-disaster reconstruction and new infrastructure, and an eleven thousand five hundred crore rupee line of credit for energy projects. The two sides signed eight agreements, covering satellite communication, policing, forensic science, health and urban development. Mr. Modi also announced an offshore I I T campus in Nepal, and the two leaders flagged off the Saryu-Kamala Express, a new weekly train between Ayodhya and Janakpur, with regular service starting November twenty-eighth.

In West Bengal, Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari says the state will be renamed Paschim Banga, in English as well. He said a resolution will be tabled in the state Assembly on December ninth and then sent to the central government.

The Supreme Court has directed tribunals hearing appeals over West Bengal's revised voter rolls to give priority to people whose names were removed, and asked the Election Commission to add more tribunals. In a separate hearing, the court said only vehicles meeting Bharat Stage Six emission norms should be allowed in Delhi during the winter, as it weighed steps against the capital's recurring air pollution.

Air India and Air India Express have extended the suspension of their flights to Saudi Arabia until November thirtieth, citing the security situation in the region after Houthi drone and missile attacks on Saudi targets.

On Dalal Street, a four-week rally came to an abrupt halt. The Sensex opened higher but then fell one thousand four hundred and eighty-four points, or one point seven seven percent, to eighty-two thousand, four hundred and thirty-six. The Nifty dropped four hundred and fifty-one points to twenty-five thousand, three hundred and fifty. The Nifty Bank index slid more than three percent. Analysts pointed to profit-taking after the recent run, worries about a possible rate hike by the U.S. Federal Reserve, and heavy selling in banking and metal stocks. The rupee ended a five-day winning streak, weakening about thirty paise to ninety-two point nine two to the dollar. Indian markets are closed on Tuesday for Guru Nanak Jayanti.

In entertainment, Ramayana has bounced back at the box office in its third weekend. Hindustan Times reports the Ranbir Kapoor and Yash film has earned seventeen hundred and eighteen crore rupees worldwide in eighteen days, making it the fastest Indian film to cross the seventeen hundred crore mark. Its overseas gross of sixty-three million dollars has also overtaken Baahubali Two as the biggest overseas haul for an Indian film that did not rely on a large release in China.

And in cricket, New Zealand batted out the final day to hold India to a draw in the first Test in Wellington. Needing eight wickets on day five, India took three, through Washington Sundar, Prasidh Krishna and Anshul Kamboj. But Daryl Mitchell, forty-nine not out, and Glenn Phillips, twenty-six not out, put on sixty-three unbeaten runs, and the captains shook hands with New Zealand at two hundred and thirty-nine for five, still one hundred and thirteen runs behind. The series moves to Christchurch for the second Test.
