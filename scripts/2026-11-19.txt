This is India News Today with Nisha. It's Thursday, November nineteenth.

Cyclone Akvan has changed course and is now headed for the Odisha coast. The India Meteorological Department says the severe cyclonic storm is likely to cross the coast between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha, close to Gopalpur, by noon on Friday, India time, with winds of ninety to one hundred kilometres an hour, gusting to one hundred and ten. That is weaker than earlier forecast. Odisha has moved more than one lakh people to shelters, most of them in Ganjam district, and deployed two hundred and ninety-five rescue teams. Schools are shut in several districts, and East Coast Railway has cancelled thirty-nine trains. In Andhra Pradesh, Srikakulam and Vizianagaram districts are on red alert for extremely heavy rain.

In Tokyo, Defence Minister Rajnath Singh and External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar held the fourth India-Japan two-plus-two dialogue with their Japanese counterparts. The two sides agreed to deepen cooperation on defence, maritime security, undersea cables, and the security of artificial intelligence systems. They will speed up a plan to let Japan's Self-Defense Forces use Indian firing ranges for live-fire training, and work toward transferring a Japanese naval antenna system, known as Unicorn, to the Indian Navy. Mr. Jaishankar warned of a growing tendency to weaponise supply chains and resources.

Bank unions representing about two and a half lakh employees and officers have called a nationwide strike for December third. They oppose the proposed sale of a sixty percent stake in IDBI Bank to Canada's Fairfax Financial Holdings, a sale they called unwarranted.

Retirement savers may soon get easier access to their money. The board of the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation has approved cutting the minimum balance that must stay in a provident fund account to ten percent, from twenty-five percent. The change still has to be notified by the Labour Ministry.

On Dalal Street, stocks bounced back sharply, led by auto and bank shares, while IT stocks fell. Maruti Suzuki jumped more than seven percent. The Sensex rose nearly nine hundred and ninety points to eighty-three thousand and twenty-one. The Nifty gained two hundred and eighty-six points to twenty-five thousand, five hundred and fourteen, and the Nifty Bank index hit a fresh high. The rupee rose sixteen paise to ninety-three point two nine to the dollar, its strongest level in seven months.

In entertainment, Ramayana Part One has set a record for the biggest second week at the Indian box office, with about two hundred and eighty crore rupees in just six days, ahead of Pushpa Two and Baahubali Two. Its India net total now stands at about eight hundred and fifty crore.

And in cricket, India won the toss and chose to bowl on a green pitch at the Basin Reserve in Wellington. Jasprit Bumrah struck in the first over and finished the day with three wickets for fifty-two, as New Zealand slumped to seventy for five. But Henry Nicholls made an unbeaten one hundred and thirteen, his twelfth Test century, and Glenn Phillips is ninety-four not out. Their unbroken stand of one hundred and seventy-three runs took New Zealand to two hundred and forty-three for five at stumps on day one. Nicholls was dropped on one hundred and two.
