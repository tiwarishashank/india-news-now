This is India News Today with Nisha. It's Wednesday, November eleventh.

Gunmen on a motorcycle opened fire on a CRPF checkpoint in Jammu and Kashmir's Kulgam district on Wednesday afternoon before fleeing. The CRPF says two of its personnel were injured, while other officials put the number at three, with one in critical condition. The Indian Express says it is the first terror attack there since July thirty-first. A search operation is under way.

In Assam, the locomotive and five coaches of the Kolkata-Sairang Express derailed near Digaru station early Wednesday. Officials say the loco pilot could not control its speed. Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma said all passengers were safe, with four suffering minor injuries, and an alternate train took them on to Mizoram.

The Union Cabinet approved three thousand, two hundred and thirty crore rupees to turn Delhi's century-old North and South Block buildings into the Yuge Yugeen Bharat Museum. The Culture Ministry says it will have the largest display area of any museum in the world. The first gallery should open in about a year, with completion targeted by twenty thirty-one. The Cabinet also cleared a two thousand, three hundred and ninety crore rupee extension of the Noida Metro's Aqua Line.

Indian stocks rallied on foreign buying and softer oil prices. The Sensex rose seven hundred and seventy-three points to eighty-one thousand, eight hundred and seventy-nine, and the Nifty gained two hundred and ten points to twenty-five thousand, two hundred and fifty-five. The rupee strengthened to ninety-three point six nine to the dollar, its strongest level since April. And Amul and Mother Dairy are raising milk prices by two to four rupees a litre from Thursday, citing higher fodder and production costs.

In entertainment, Ramayana Part One keeps breaking records. Hindustan Times reports the film netted ninety-four crore rupees in India on Tuesday, the biggest Tuesday ever, taking its six-day domestic net to four hundred and forty crore. Its worldwide gross now stands at eight hundred and twenty-three crore.

In cricket, Mumbai Indians and Punjab Kings have completed one of the biggest trades in IPL history. Hardik Pandya, who captained Mumbai for three seasons, moves to Punjab for eighteen crore rupees, and left-arm pacer Arshdeep Singh heads to Mumbai at the same fee. Arshdeep took one hundred and eleven wickets in ninety-six matches for Punjab. And off the field, he announced his engagement to Samreen Kaur in New Zealand, where India's one-day series resumes on November thirteenth.
