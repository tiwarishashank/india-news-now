A new storm is forming in the Bay of Bengal, and the India Meteorological Department says it is likely to become a cyclonic storm on Monday and a severe cyclonic storm on Tuesday, then move toward the Andhra Pradesh coast. Once it becomes a cyclone, it will be called Akvan, a name proposed by Iran. The Indian Express says it would be the third cyclone to approach the state in less than a month, after Arnab and Murasu. On Sunday morning the depression was about nine hundred and sixty kilometres south-southeast of Visakhapatnam. Fishermen have been warned to stay off the sea next week, and coastal Andhra Pradesh and Odisha have been cautioned about intense rain.

Prince William and Kate Middleton will visit Mumbai from Monday to Wednesday for the Earthshot Prize awards. The Indian Express, citing PTI, says they will announce this year's five winners on Tuesday. The prize, founded by Prince William six years ago, awards one million pounds to each winner. Organisers called this year's event the biggest yet, after work with fifteen thousand schools across twenty-four Indian states.

Air India has restored non-stop flights from Delhi and Mumbai to New York, after some airspace restrictions eased. Hindustan Times reports the airline has dropped technical refuelling stops in Rome on all four India to New York routes, cutting travel time by up to five hours, from about twenty-two hours to around seventeen. It already flies daily from both cities to Kennedy Airport, and several times a week to Newark. Pakistan's airspace remains closed to Indian airlines.

Delhi's air stayed in the very poor category on Sunday morning, with an overall Air Quality Index of three hundred and fifty. Of fifty-four monitoring stations, forty-seven were very poor and seven were severe. IIT Delhi recorded the highest reading, at four hundred and twenty-eight. The weather office forecast a cloudy sky, with light rain or drizzle.

In cricket, India have won the one-day international series in New Zealand, their first there in seven years. At the Bay Oval in Tauranga, they defended two hundred and nine and beat New Zealand by twenty-two runs, taking the series three-one. The Indian Express says Mohammed Siraj, playing his first match of the series, and Arshdeep Singh led the bowling. Arshdeep took three for thirty, and Siraj took two for fifty. Axar Patel made forty-six and took two wickets, including the last. Rohit Sharma was out for seven and Virat Kohli for five. The BCCI says New Zealand were bowled out for one hundred and eighty-seven. The two sides now turn to Tests, starting at the Basin Reserve on November nineteenth.
