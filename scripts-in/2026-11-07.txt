The BJP is reaching out to Sharad Pawar and his NCP-SP faction as it looks to build support for fresh delimitation and women's reservation bills that need a two-thirds majority in Parliament. Prime Minister Narendra Modi recently phoned Pawar in hospital, and Home Minister Amit Shah met an NCP delegation in Delhi. Maharashtra Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis said any merger of the two NCP factions is for the Pawars to decide, though the NCP would consult the BJP as an NDA partner. Talks gained momentum after NCP working president Praful Patel visited Pawar in Mumbai, with Pawar saying they would sit down and discuss the matter after November tenth.

Separately, Congress leader Rahul Gandhi attacked the Modi government over E20 petrol on Saturday, citing Karnataka government fuel-sample tests and accusing the Centre of shortchanging motorists on the actual ethanol content in fuel sold nationwide.

On markets, Indian stocks closed Friday with sharp gains, extending their winning streak to a seventh session even as global markets sold off. The Sensex jumped one thousand seven hundred and eighteen points, or two point two percent, to eighty-one thousand and twenty-eight. The Nifty rose four hundred and ninety-four points, or two percent, to twenty-five thousand and thirty, reclaiming the twenty-five thousand mark. Banks led the rally amid festive optimism ahead of Diwali. Foreign institutional investors bought a net thirteen thousand seven hundred and fifty crore rupees of Indian equities on Friday alone, and about forty-six thousand crore over the week. The rupee surged fifty-two paise to close at ninety-three point eight nine to the dollar, helped by a softer greenback and firm domestic markets.

In entertainment, Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana Part One collected about forty-eight crore rupees net in India on its second day, taking its domestic net past one hundred and thirteen crore and its worldwide gross past two hundred and twenty-one crore. Ranbir Kapoor plays Lord Rama, with Sai Pallavi as Sita and Yash as Ravana.

In cricket, India beat New Zealand by four runs under the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern method in a rain-hit second ODI in Wellington, taking a two-nil lead in the five-match series. New Zealand were bowled out for one hundred and thirty-four, with Gurnoor Brar taking four wickets, and India were one hundred and thirteen for seven when rain ended play. Separately, Mohammed Shami announced his retirement from Test cricket after sixty-four matches and two hundred and twenty-nine wickets. He will continue in white-ball cricket. BCCI vice president Rajeev Shukla said the decision was Shami's alone.
