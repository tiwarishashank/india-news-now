This is India News Today with Nisha. It's Friday, November twentieth.

Cyclone Akvan has crossed the coast near Gopalpur in south Odisha, and officials say there have been no reports of deaths. The storm made landfall between seven thirty and eight thirty on Friday morning, India time, with winds of seventy to eighty kilometres an hour, gusting to ninety, and has since weakened into a deep depression. Parts of Ganjam district got around two hundred millimetres of rain, and some roads in Berhampur were flooded. Odisha had moved more than three lakh seventy thousand people to shelters, and more than forty-one trains were cancelled.

In Rajasthan, the Congress has dealt a setback to the ruling BJP in panchayat elections. With most results declared on Friday, the Election Commission's website showed the Congress with more than three thousand three hundred Panchayat Samiti seats, against about two thousand for the BJP. State Congress chief Govind Singh Dotasra called it a sign that the BJP government has lost the public's trust.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi had blunt words for Indian business. Speaking at the centenary convention of the industry body FICCI in New Delhi, he said corporate tax had been cut from thirty percent to twenty-two percent, but companies were not raising salaries or hiring more people. He told industry leaders their appetite was so insatiable that no amount was ever enough, and said he felt ashamed that Indian consumers were not getting quality products.

Pakistan's climate change minister, Musadik Malik, has arrived in New Delhi for the first Shanghai Cooperation Organisation Civilisational Dialogue Forum, which runs through November twenty-second. Hindustan Times reports he is the first Pakistani leader to visit India in more than three years. He called the meeting a tremendous opportunity for multilateral engagement.

India has also unveiled its first home-grown vaccine to protect chickens from H5N1 bird flu. Developed by a government animal disease laboratory in Bhopal, it is priced at one to two rupees a dose.

On Dalal Street, stocks rose for a second straight day, led by banks, as foreign investors kept buying and oil prices cooled. The Sensex gained eight hundred and ninety-eight points to eighty-three thousand, nine hundred and twenty. The Nifty rose two hundred and eighty-six points to twenty-five thousand, eight hundred and one, and the Nifty Bank index closed at another record high. The rupee jumped sixty-two paise to ninety-two point six seven to the dollar, a seven-month high.

In entertainment, Ramayana Part One has overtaken Dhurandhar to enter the list of the five highest-grossing Indian films worldwide. The Indian Express puts its global total at about fifteen hundred and forty-seven crore rupees after fifteen days.

And in cricket, India have hit back in the first Test in Wellington. New Zealand were bowled out for three hundred and sixty-eight, with Henry Nicholls unbeaten on one hundred and seventy-five and Glenn Phillips making one hundred and eighteen. Then Yashasvi Jaiswal hit eighty-five off eighty-nine balls, putting on one hundred and thirty for the first wicket with K. L. Rahul, who is fifty-two not out. India closed day two at one hundred and fifty-nine for one, trailing by two hundred and nine runs.
