Cyclone Murasu has made landfall on India's east coast. The India Meteorological Department says the severe cyclonic storm crossed near Ongole in Andhra Pradesh's Prakasam district in the early hours of Tuesday, with winds of one hundred to one hundred and ten kilometres an hour, gusting to one hundred and twenty. By afternoon it had weakened into a cyclonic storm. Officials report hundreds of trees uprooted, electricity poles damaged, and crop losses, but no human casualties so far. More than two thousand people were moved to relief shelters. It is the second cyclone to hit the region in a week, after Cyclone Arnab.

In politics, Home Minister Amit Shah said the government is ready to call a special session of Parliament to debate the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls, and challenged Congress leader Rahul Gandhi to take part. Congress hit back, saying Shah should first secure the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and suspend the revision. Separately, Srinagar Lok Sabha MP Aga Syed Ruhullah Mehdi quit the National Conference and resigned his seat, accusing Chief Minister Omar Abdullah's government of abandoning campaign promises, including the restoration of Article three seventy.

Indian stocks extended their winning run for a fourth straight session. The Sensex jumped one thousand, two hundred and twenty-five points, or one point six percent, to close at seventy-six thousand, nine hundred and eighteen. The Nifty rose three hundred and seventy points to twenty-three thousand, eight hundred and eleven. Both closed near two-month highs, helped by foreign buying. The rupee gained for a third day, settling near ninety-four point seven five to the dollar.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated Pune Metro's twenty-three kilometre Pink Line, built at more than eight thousand, three hundred crore rupees, and laid the foundation for four new corridors. He said India's operational metro network has grown from two hundred and forty-eight kilometres before twenty fourteen to twelve hundred kilometres today.

In entertainment, the Bombay High Court granted interim anti-piracy protection to Ranbir Kapoor's Ramayana ahead of its November fifth worldwide release. The court restrained unauthorized recording and streaming of the film, which the makers say cost about four thousand crore rupees across both parts.

In cricket, India face New Zealand in the first of five one-day internationals Wednesday at Eden Park in Auckland. Captain Shubman Gill said injured all-rounder Hardik Pandya, who has not played since the IPL, will need to play domestic cricket before returning to the national side. Fast bowler Prasidh Krishna has been cleared fit for all formats after a hamstring injury, though he will miss the ODIs and may join the Test squad later this month.
