This is India News Today with Nisha. It's Saturday, November twenty-first.

At least thirteen people have died and more than three hundred have been taken to hospital after drinking spurious liquor in Manipur's Chandel district, police said on Saturday. The illness began on November eighteenth. Most of the patients are from Chakpikarong and nearby areas, and they are being treated at several hospitals in Imphal.

In New Delhi, a fast-track court has formally framed charges against all fourteen people accused in the NEET undergraduate paper leak case. The leak forced the cancellation of the medical entrance exam in May. The accused pleaded not guilty before Special Judge Ajay Gupta and will face trial.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi was in Bihar on Saturday, where he inaugurated the Nitish Setu bridge in Vaishali district and waved a gamcha, recreating his celebration from the National Democratic Alliance's sweeping win in last year's state election. He later addressed the seventy-fifth anniversary celebrations of I I T Kharagpur, the first I I T of independent India, and urged students to break free of limiting beliefs.

The B J P has seized on praise from President Donald Trump, who said at a White House briefing that India is growing at seven percent. B J P leader Ashish Sood said the world was recognising India's progress while Congress leader Rahul Gandhi was turning a blind eye to it.

New data show output in India's eight core industries grew ten point nine percent in October, the fastest pace in the new series, led by electricity, coal, cement and iron ore. Economists noted a weak base from a year earlier helped lift the number.

Indian stocks have now risen for four weeks in a row, their longest weekly winning streak since July, helped by record foreign buying and cooling oil prices. For the week, the Sensex gained two point eight five percent to close Friday at eighty-three thousand, nine hundred and twenty. The Nifty rose two point six one percent to twenty-five thousand, eight hundred and one. Public sector bank shares led the rally. The rupee ended the week at ninety-two point six seven to the dollar, a seven-month high.

In entertainment, Ranbir Kapoor's Ramayana is facing a protest from the Vishwa Hindu Parishad. Its leaders have demanded that screenings be stopped and the film's censor certificate revoked, alleging that the demon king Ravana is glorified and facts are distorted. The film remains the biggest draw at the box office in its third week.

And in cricket, India have taken control of the first Test in Wellington. K. L. Rahul made one hundred and thirty-six, becoming the first Indian opener to score Test centuries in all four SENA countries: South Africa, England, New Zealand and Australia. Captain Shubman Gill was bowled for ninety-eight in the last over of the day, and Rishabh Pant is fifty-five not out. India closed day three at four hundred and eighty-six for four, a lead of one hundred and eighteen runs over New Zealand.
